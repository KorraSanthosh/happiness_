# Where does the remaining error come from? — Verified investigation of the GlucoTwin v7 model

> Generated by `backend/app/verify_results.py --update-artifacts` from `models/investigation/analysis.json`
> after all automated consistency checks passed (`models/investigation/verified_metrics.json`). Do not edit numbers by hand.

## Evaluation protocol (applies to every figure unless stated)

- **Predictions:** v7 held-out predictions (`models/combined_v7/oof_*.parquet`), 7-fold **person-grouped** nested
  cross-validation over **187 people**. Every prediction comes from a model that never saw that person.
- **Unit:** one 15-minute *prediction moment* with a real CGM reading and a known label. The 2-hour horizon has **530,807** moments.
- **Label:** 1 if the maximum CGM reading in the next 2 h is > 180 mg/dL. Re-deriving every label from the raw glucose reproduced all of them exactly.
- **Decision threshold:** accuracy mode, chosen per fold on inner folds only (median 0.51).
- **No model was changed for this report.** No new algorithm was tried.

## 1. Accuracy and label reliability (2-hour horizon)

| Population | Moments | Accuracy | Moments near 180* | Accuracy, clear outcome | Accuracy, outcome near 180 | Share of errors near 180 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All 187 people | 530,807 | **84.9 %** | 22.8 % | 90.2 % | 66.8 % | 49.9 % |
| Type 2 (100 people) | 110,862 | **89.8 %** | 19.9 % | 94.4 % | 71.1 % | 56.4 % |

\* "Near 180" means the **actual** 2-h peak was 162–198 mg/dL (180 ± 10 %, a typical-CGM-error assumption, not measured on these sensors). The split
is made using the true outcome, so it **decomposes the error**; it is not a subset the model could pick in advance.
Overall accuracy is the weighted average of the two columns, for example 22.8 % × 66.8 % + 77.2 % × 90.2 % = 84.9 %.

## 2. How noisy is the reference itself? CGM vs fingerstick (Shanghai only)

- **Data:** ShanghaiT1DM/T2DM files: 'CGM (mg / dl)' and 'CBG (mg / dl)' (capillary blood glucose) columns. **4,010 pairs from 98 people** (median 37 pairs per person).
- **Pairing:** A CBG value is recorded on a row of the 15-min CGM grid; that row's CGM value is its pair. The true fingerstick time is not given, so time alignment is only to within the grid step.
- **Method:** relative difference (CGM - CBG) / CBG per pair; MARD = mean |diff|; bias = median diff; duplicate timestamps dropped; CBG outside 40-600 mg/dL dropped; 95 % CI by resampling people.

| | Pairs | People | MARD | Median abs. difference (95 % CI) | Median bias (95 % CI) | Within ±15 % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All | 4,010 | 98 | 13.9 % | 11.0 % (9.6 to 12.5) | -7.0 % (-10.6 to -3.8) | 63.6 % |
| Type 2 | 3,261 | 88 | 12.8 % | 10.2 % | -5.8 % | 66.6 % |
| Type 1 | 749 | 10 | 18.4 % | 14.8 % | -12.6 % | 50.5 % |

A negative bias means the CGM reads lower than the fingerstick. **Caveats:** Fingersticks were taken at clinical times (often around meals), not at random moments. Glucose meters have their own error (ISO 15197 allows ±15 %), so this is CGM-meter disagreement, not the CGM's true error. Applies to the Shanghai sensors only; the other datasets use different CGM devices and have no reference readings. The ±10 % band in section 1
is a typical-CGM assumption; it is not derived from these pairs.

## 3. Missed spikes (2-hour horizon)

**Serious spike:** peak >= 250 mg/dL OR >= 3 consecutive bins above 180.

| Counting unit | Serious spikes | Missed | Miss rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distinct episodes, warning required 30–120 min before onset (clinically meaningful) | 9,503 | 3,495 | **36.8 %** (95 % CI 34.2 % to 39.7 %, by person) |
| Prediction moments (overlapping windows; one spike counted many times) | 146,990 | 34,936 | 23.8 % |

- **Episode rule:** Distinct hyperglycaemia episodes: a run of consecutive 15-min bins with a reading > 180, preceded by an observed bin <= 180. An episode is WARNED if any held-out 2-h prediction made 30-120 min before onset (bins onset-8 .. onset-2, while glucose was <= 180) was at or above its fold threshold. Episodes with no held-out prediction in that window are not evaluable and are counted separately. 344 of 12,879 episodes had no held-out prediction in the warning window and are excluded.
- **All spike episodes:** 38.4 % missed (4,809 of 12,535). For warned serious episodes, the latest warning came a median of 30 min before onset.
- **What the misses look like (moment level):** glucose at prediction time had a median of 131.4 mg/dL for misses vs 207.2 for spikes caught.
  55.1 % of misses started more than 60 min later (vs 4.4 %).
  Where meals are logged, 37.1 % of missed moments had a meal logged **after** the prediction (vs 22.1 %).
- **Limitation:** Alarm burden is not captured here: a warning counts even if many other alarms were false.

## 4. Calibration (held-out predictions)

Method: Held-out (out-of-fold) probabilities; ECE = 10 equal-width bins weighted by row count; slope/intercept from logistic regression of y on logit(p); 95 % CI by resampling people.

| Horizon | Moments | ECE (95 % CI) | Calibration slope | People with ≥ 200 moments | Median per-person gap | People off by > 5 points |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30m | 526,103 | 0.15 % (0.12 % to 0.34 %) | 1.013 | 186 | 0.5 % | 0.5 % |
| 60m | 534,206 | 0.34 % (0.27 % to 0.55 %) | 1.023 | 186 | 1.2 % | 1.6 % |
| 2h | 530,807 | 0.38 % (0.34 % to 0.98 %) | 1.031 | 186 | 2.2 % | 12.4 % |

**Limitations:** Rows within a person are autocorrelated, so row counts overstate the effective sample size; CIs resample people to account for this. These are the 7 fold models' predictions pooled, not the final model trained on all people. Per-person gaps use people with >= 200 rows.

## 5. Patient-level generalisation (2-hour horizon, people with ≥ 200 moments)

- **Beats the "already above 180" rule:** 91.9 % of 186 people.
- **Per-person accuracy** (5th / 50th / 95th percentile): 78.2 % / 86.7 % / 96.8 %.
- **Per-person AUC** (same percentiles): 0.837 / 0.908 / 0.970.
- **Within each dataset**, people with more frequent spikes have lower accuracy but not lower AUC (Spearman correlation of AUC with spike prevalence is positive in all six datasets, significant in three: `within_dataset_spearman` in `analysis.json`). Their outcomes are closer to 50/50 rather than ranked worse.

## 6. Conclusions

1. **Calibration** is good on held-out people (ECE ≤ 0.38 %).
2. **About half of the 2-h errors** (49.9 %) occur where the actual peak was within ±10 % of 180 mg/dL.
   Where a reference exists, CGM and fingerstick differ by a median of 11.0 % (MARD 13.9 %).
3. **Distinct serious episodes:** 36.8 % get no warning 30–120 min ahead. Missed spikes typically start from normal glucose and later in the window,
   often after a meal logged *after* the prediction time.
4. **Next steps** are new information known at prediction time: meal announcements, reference checks, and an Indian cohort. A different algorithm is not the next step.
